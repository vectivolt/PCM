## 0. The architecture in one page

```
                    reinforced barrier B1 (the only one)
   PE-referenced / SELV  ║   DC side ("live"), everything referenced to BUS-
  ───────────────────────╫──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   CAN (BMS/PCS)  ◄──────╫── CA-IS3062W ──┐
   RS-485         ◄──────╫── CA-IS3082W ──┤
   ENABLE / E-stop ──────╫── CA-IS3821L ──┼── F280039C (C2000, live) ── trip LATCH ── PWM gating ──► 12/16 NSI6651 channels
   STATUS out     ◄──────╫── CA-IS3821L ──┤        │  ADC / CMPSS              ▲          (functional insulation,
   3 fans (24 V)  ◄──────╫── fan buck  ◄──┤        │                           │           per-phase bias transformer)
   SELV 24 V      ◄──────╫── AUX-T1 SELV winding (reinforced)                  │
                         ║   AUX-T1 live winding 24 V ─► coils, 5 V, 3.3 V     │ OC / OV / OT / DESAT / RDY / WD / STOP
                         ║   flyback primary ◄── diode-OR of A+ and B+ terminal sides (starts at 200-220 V)
  ───────────────────────╫──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                    basic barrier B2: DC side ↔ earthed heatsink / chassis / PE
```

- **Power stage unchanged:** 3 (4) interleaved two-level FSBB phases, 2 × SG2M040170HJ per switch, 32 kHz, NSI6651
  channel rev 6, inductor rev M1, control and MPPT as designed.
- **Controller on BUS-:** one F280039C (4.40 USD) on a small control board; dividers, shunts and TMR sensors without
  isolated amplifiers; **one reinforced barrier** (communication, enable/stop, status, fans, SELV supply).
- **Self-powered:** one 75 W flyback (AUX-HV topology) from both ports' terminal sides, starting at 200-220 V; a
  reinforced SELV winding feeds fans and communication; no cabinet 24 V.
- **Ports:** PV port = varistor network + **one contactor** (+ pole), no fuse, no precharge. Battery port = varistor
  network + **one aR fuse per pole** + one contactor + precharge relay/resistor. No clamp banks.
- **One hardware latch** (no dual channel) with OC, OV, OT, DESAT, gate-supply loss, watchdog and external stop.
- **Two boards:** power board (~1,615 parts) + control board (~236 parts) = **~1,850 parts** (PV-P75).
- **BOM at catalogue / 1 k prices: 966 USD (12.9 USD/kW)** for PV-P75, **1,177 USD** for PV-P100/110 — 65 % below
  today's 2,789 USD, but **21 % above the 800 USD budget**. Chinese-volume view (stated factors, §14): ~736 USD.
- **What closes the gap:** the frozen inductor (240 USD = 25 % of the BOM at an 80 USD estimate each) has to be
  re-opened for cost; nothing else of that size is left that does not cost safety (§14).

**What disappears:** the 132 audited HV-PELV barriers of the old platform (12 UCC14241, 12 NSI6651 as safety
barriers, AMC3330/AMC3302, TPSI3050, contactor coils, CNY65B, AUX-HV T1, LA 150-P …) become functional; the 22

### 2.2 The hard questions

- **Fans.** They cannot be live: the fan's own dielectric rating is **500 V AC 1 min frame to + lead** and the leads
  are UL1007 300 V (Delta AFB1224SHE-F00 spec p.2, `docs/datasheets/thermal/AFB1224SHE-F00.pdf`), against 2200 V rms
  needed for basic insulation of a 1000 V DC circuit. The fans are therefore on the SELV side: powered from the
  reinforced SELV winding of AUX-T1 through a 7-24 V fan buck on the control board, speed command and tachs across
  B1 (isolator channels). Fan frames are plastic (UL94V-0) and mounted on the earthed chassis; the fan's internal
  insulation is only functional, which is acceptable for an SELV circuit.
- **Communication ports.** CAN and RS-485 transceivers are isolated (reinforced, B1); their bus sides, the
  terminations, TVS (SM712-class to SELV 0 V, SMBJ to PE) and the connectors sit in the SELV zone at the front panel.
  The BMS / PCS side may earth its 0 V; the SELV 0 V is tied to PE through 1 MΩ ‖ 4.7 nF Y2 so a solid bond by the
  installer (PELV) is harmless.
- **Enable / emergency-stop input.** A dry contact (or 24 V SELV signal) in the SELV zone drives a reverse isolator
  channel of the **default-low** variant: SELV supply lost, wire broken or contact open = STOP. The isolator output
  sets the hardware latch (§6) directly — firmware is not in the path. It is a single-channel functional stop, not a
  SIL/PL-rated safety function (§12).
- **Service / debug connector.** The JTAG/cJTAG header is on the **live** zone, inside the enclosure only, marked
  "HAZARDOUS VOLTAGE — isolated debug probe only (≥ 1000 V DC rated, e.g. an isolated XDS)". Field service and
  firmware update go over CAN (SELV) with a CAN bootloader: no live access is needed in the field. This is the
  normal arrangement for this product class (COST-REVIEW §4).
- **Heatsink.** **Earthed (PE)**, as D-032 froze it. The alternative — heatsink on BUS- — would cut the common-mode
  source (the switch-node tabs see the heatsink through ~24 pF each) and allow thinner functional pads, but it puts a
  large live metal part in the PD3 air path (16 mm creepage standoffs, IPXXB at both air openings) and is a trap for
  service staff who expect an earthed heatsink. Rejected on safety; the EMI cost of the earthed heatsink is one
  nanocrystalline core per port (§8).
- **What the earthed enclosure must provide.** (1) A PE stud with ≥ 16 mm² Cu (line conductors 35-50 mm²) and the
  heatsink bonded to it at two points (≥ 6 mm² each); (2) IP20 / IPXXB for every live part with the covers closed,
  covers removable only with a tool; (3) the label "Hazardous voltage — wait 10 min (PV-P100/110: 15 min) after
  disconnection" (passive bleeders, §8); (4) DC terminals at the rear with touch covers and PD3 creepage ≥ 16 mm;
  SELV connectors at the front, ≥ 10 mm from any live part; (5) the cooling-air path separated from the coated PD2
  electronics compartment (D-032); (6) the PE return for the Y capacitors, MOV3 and the IMD strings (removable strap
  for the hipot, as port.py's PE_T).

## 3. Auxiliary supply

**Topology:** single-switch DCM flyback, the AUX-HV rev C1 circuit (`gen/aux_hv.py`): UCC28C59-Q1 at 64.9 kHz, 48 %
duty limit, one 1700 V SiC switch (IV2Q171R0D7Z / SG2M1K0170J2J), BSS126 cascode start-up, TPS3710 brown-in,
TLV3202 input-OV lockout + overload timer, TVS clamp. **Changes:** rating 30 W → **75 W continuous** (new transformer
AUX-T1, EC41/ETD39 class; first estimate Lp ≈ 0.7 mH, Ipk ≈ 2.0 A at 200 V, to be designed with
`sim/aux_hv_design.py` and `sim/magnetics.py`); the primary and the controller are referenced to BUS-, so the live
24 V winding is regulated **directly** (divider into the UCC28C59 FB pin — no optocoupler, no TL431); the second secondary is the
reinforced SELV winding.

**Input:** one tap per port on the terminal side (before K_A / K_B): 2 × 22 Ω pulse-rated wirewound + 1 A 1000 V DC
fuse + 1600 V diode each (the AUX-HV input block), diode-OR, 2 × 3.3 µF 1300 V film surge absorber. A reversed port
is blocked by its diode. Brown-in 220 V rising / 200 V falling → the module wakes at ≤ 250 V on either port (PV-C2).

## 6. Protection and safety concept

### 6.1 Trip sources → one latch

| source | detection | threshold | path (no firmware) | gates off after the event |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| inductor over-current, per phase | TMR → window comparator | ±72.2 A (band 68.5-76 A) | comparator → latch → AND gates on all PWM lines + all drivers' EN | ≤ 1.5 µs (TMR 0.2-0.3 µs + comparator 0.3 µs + logic + NSI6651 90 ns + gate fall); required ≤ 8.9 µs (control_spec local layer) |
| inductor over-current, backup | F280039C CMPSS (one per phase, H + L) → ePWM trip zone | ±92 A (84-98.6 A) | on-chip, configured at boot | ≤ 1.3 µs; required ≤ 4.87 µs |
| short circuit / shoot-through | NSI6651 DESAT (9.26 V, 465 ns blanking) + rev-6 booster | per channel | driver soft turn-off; FLT_N (wired-OR) → latch → all other channels | 0.62 µs (frozen channel) |
| port over-voltage A and B | bank-side divider → comparator | 1100 V (software limit 1050 V) | → latch | ≤ 35 µs (required ≤ 58.5 µs) |
| over-temperature | heatsink NTCs → comparator; inductor NTCs → comparator | heatsink 95 °C; inductor 145 °C (10 K below the 155 °C hot-spot limit) | → latch | thermal |
| gate-drive supply lost | NSI6651 RDY (UVLO either side, wired-AND); 5 V / 24 V supervisor | UVLO | → latch | ≤ 1 µs |
| watchdog | 74LVC1G123 heartbeat monoflop (ISR toggles a pin; 5 ms) + TPS3823 reset (1.6 s) | 5 ms | → latch | 5 ms |
| external stop | SELV contact → default-low isolator channel | open contact / lost SELV supply | → latch | ≤ 2 ms (debounce) |
| reverse polarity / precharge | VAX, VBX comparators | VBX < +200 V, ΔV > 10 V | blocks the contactor coil drivers (hardware AND) | — |

**The latch:** one set-dominant flip-flop (74LVC1G74) with a diode/gate OR of all sources. Output: (1) AND gates
(3-4 × 74LVC08) on every PWM line, (2) all NSI6651 RST/EN low (latches their fault state, ≥ 0.8 µs), (3) the MCU trip
zone (INPUT X-BAR) and a GPIO, (4) the contactor logic (§6.2), (5) the status output. It can be cleared only by a
firmware pulse **and** only while no source is active. No dual channel, no fault analysis (D-044).

### 6.2 Contactor rules (K_A, K_B, K_PRE)

1. **Close** only with the latch clear, the terminal voltage of the right polarity (VAX/VBX > +200 V — hardware
   interlock in the coil driver, so firmware cannot close onto a reversed source), the IMD result ≥ 33 kΩ before K_A
   (IEC 62109-2 V/30 mA rule — from memory) and |V_bank − V_terminal| ≤ 10 V for K_B (after precharge, the
   `port_spec` criterion). K_A closes after the converter has pre-charged the A bank from port B (ΔV ≤ 20 V) when
   the battery is present; on a PV-only start it closes onto the empty bank, limited by the array (≤ 169 A) — the
   making current (Isc plus the discharge of the array/cable capacitance, ~0.5 kA estimate) must be confirmed with
   Hongfa (R-04).
2. **Open** for a normal stop only after the converter has stopped and |I| < 300 A has held for ≥ 20 ms (two release
   times): HFE82V-300C/1000 is rated for 200 openings at 300 A / 1000 V.
3. **Fault opening up to 1.0 kA:** the hardware hold-off keeps the coil energised while |I_port| ≥ 1.0 kA (band
   0.95-1.05 kA: shunt 1 % + comparator + reference). The hold can only keep an energised coil on; it can never
   energise an open contactor. The contactor's one-time breaking capacity is 1500 A at 1000 V ("no fire, no
   explosion", HFE82V data sheet), so 1.05 kA keeps a 1.4× margin. Every opening above 300 A flags the contactor for
   replacement.
4. **No auxiliary contact** (HFE82V has none): a weld is found by moving the bank voltage ≥ 50 V after opening and
   checking that VAX/VBX does not follow; a weld blocks restart.

### 6.3 Battery port coordination and the installation requirement

| current fed from the battery into the module | who clears it |
|---|---|
| ≤ 300 A | converter control / K_B (normal) |
| 300 A – 0.95 kA | K_B opens (fault opening, contactor replaced afterwards) |
| **0.95 kA – 1.3 kA** | **neither guaranteed in the module** — K_B holds, the HPE501 250 A aR fuse may melt but is rated to break only from 5 In = 1.25 kA (+10 % curve tolerance) |
| 1.25 kA – 50 kA | HPE501/000B100-250 per pole (aR, 1000 V DC, L/R 1-3 ms; clearing I²t ≤ 85 kA²s) |

**Installation requirement (battery / DC-bus side), stated as numbers:**
- The upstream battery protection (rack fuse, DC breaker or BMS-controlled rack contactor) must interrupt **any
  current between 0.95 kA and 1.3 kA flowing into the module's battery port within 0.25 s** (the HPE501 250 A
  pre-arc time at 1.375 kA, i.e. 1.25 kA + 10 % tolerance, read from its curve; the contactor carries 1000 A for 25 s).
- The prospective short-circuit current at the module's battery terminals must not exceed **50 kA with L/R ≤ 3 ms**.
- The cable from the battery protection to the module is protected by that upstream device (the module fuses protect
  the module, not the external cable).
- One active insulation monitor per galvanically connected system: PV array and battery are one system through
  BUS- (§5, port.py rule kept).

**PV side:** array Isc at the module terminals ≤ 1.25 × 135 A = 169 A (225 A for PV-P100/110); string fuses in the
combiner per IEC 62548; a load-break DC isolator upstream for maintenance; the array floating (IT) or one pole earthed
in agreement with the IMD.

### 6.4 Reverse polarity, terminal shorts, residual risks

- **Reverse polarity (both ports, no clamp diodes):** the + pole contactor stays open, the aux tap's diode blocks, the
  varistor pairs (2 × V1mA 750 V) do not conduct at 1000 V, so no current flows; VAX/VBX read negative → alarm.
  Firmware cannot close onto it (hardware interlock 6.2-1).
- **Bolted short at a port's terminals (no clamp banks):** the bank (270 µF, 135 J at 1000 V) dumps through the
  contactor (≈ 22 kA peak, weld likely) and rings below zero; the leg body diodes carry up to ≈ 4.5 kA per device
  (`report.md` §7) → **the port-side legs are likely destroyed**. On the battery port the dump (≈ 39 kA²s) also melts
  the aR fuses (pre-arc 5.3 kA²s); the battery's own current flows in the external short, outside the module, and is
  the upstream protection's job; the PV array keeps feeding an external PV short (current-limited, the installation's
  matter). The module then stays latched and is repaired. It is not expected to burn: a PV-fed internal short is
  opened by K_A (≤ 169 A ≪ 1.0 kA); a battery-fed one by the fuses.
- **Residual:** total loss of both port voltages (hence of the aux supply) while > 1 kA flows → the contactor opens
  above its rating. This needs a bolted terminal short (then I ≫ 1.25 kA and the fuse clears in < 10 ms, before the
  contactor releases in ~50 ms) — the same class of residual as D-033's "double fault".

### 6.5 Hardware / firmware allocation (owner instructions 2026-10-04 / 05, decision D-050)

The owner: "don't try to handle everything in the hardware … offload whatever possible to the firmware to save cost …
a sweet balance between hardware and software for edge cases", then: "but if it's not a significant saving, keep it",
then: "decide yourself everything".

**Decision: the hardware protections of §6.1 stay.** Moving them into the controller was worked out part by part and
would save about 17 USD per module (control board about 5, the two ports about 11, the dead-time stretch about 1.5)
— roughly 2 % of the bill of materials — and what it gives up is not worth that:

| Protection | Cost of keeping it | Why it stays |
|---|---|---|
| Discrete per-phase over-current comparators, port over-voltage comparators, over-temperature comparators, the one latch, the gating on the PWM lines, heartbeat watchdog (control board) | about 5 USD | An independent layer that works with the controller mis-programmed; the controller's own comparators remain the backup |
| RC dead-time stretch per gate-drive channel | about 1.5 USD | With it removed, a firmware dead time of zero gives about 85 ns of overlap per edge (about 59 mJ) that ends before the driver's DESAT blanking: nothing catches it and the switches fail within milliseconds (`gen/gdrv.py` rev 9) |
| Negative-rail detector per channel | about 0.03 USD per channel | A lost negative rail gives +5.1 V at the die, 1.57 mJ per event, about 50 W at 32 kHz — survivable but never seen by DESAT |
| Polarity and precharge ΔV interlocks in the coil drivers, port over-current comparators (power board) | about 11 USD for both ports | A firmware error here closes a contactor onto reversed polarity or a large voltage difference, or lets a battery-fed fault melt a fuse link below its breaking range: destroyed devices or a welded contactor |
| Contactor hold-off comparator, external stop onto the latch, DESAT, driver under-voltage lock-out, default-off pull-downs | — | Must work with the controller dead; a hazard otherwise |

**Where the rule does apply:** no new hardware is added for a rare edge case unless firmware demonstrably cannot
cover it; sequencing, monitoring, plausibility checks, temperature-dependent limits, the insulation monitor's
algorithm, fan control and every "what if" that is not a hazard are firmware. Firmware also duplicates each hardware
trip as a second layer (thresholds, timing, a start-up self-test of each trip path) — listed in
`sim/out/port_design/port_spec.json` (`lean.firmware_requirements`), the control board's design check and
`sim/out/pv_control/control_spec.json`. The lean-port functions and the gate-drive channel keep the lighter
variants as options (`interlocks="hold"`, `oc_trip=False`, `stretch=False`, `neg_det=False`) should a later cost
round want them.

### 8.1 Common-mode filter re-derived for 150 kHz – 30 MHz

**Source:** the switch-node device tabs (S2 drain = node A, S4 drain = node B) face the earthed heatsink through the
AlN pad: 24 pF per device (AlN εr 8.8, 3.1 cm², 1 mm), 58 pF per switching node with the board and inductor (10 pF
assumed). Node swing 1000 V, 124 V/ns. **Calculated** (scratch FFT, three phases at 120°, Y caps 2 × 9.4 nF, 150 Ω CM
network, no choke): worst single harmonic **106-112 dBµV** at 150-300 kHz (duty-dependent; interleaving cancels the
non-triple harmonics), ≤ 93 dBµV from 0.5 to 30 MHz.

**Limit:** the DC power port of class A group 1 equipment in CISPR 11 (GCPC, 20-75 kVA row), *from memory, verify*:
QP 116 → 106 dBµV and AV 106 → 96 dBµV (0.15-0.5 MHz), QP 106 / AV 96 dBµV above. Against the AV line (~103 dBµV at
288 kHz) the estimate is up to ~10 dB over → **requirement: ≥ 16 dB of CM attenuation at 150-500 kHz (10 dB + 6 dB
margin), ≥ 6 dB above 0.5 MHz.** Solution: one nanocrystalline ring (~63 × 38 × 25 mm) around both conductors of each
port, single turn, |Z| ≥ 100 Ω from 150 kHz to 30 MHz, plus the existing Y caps; footprint and space for a second core
or a 2-turn version. If the ≤ 20 kVA row (AV 84 → 76 dBµV) were imposed, ~30 dB would be needed: a wound 2-3 turn CM
choke per port (+20-30 USD) — decide with the first pre-compliance scan.

**Leakage-current limit (stated):** the module's common-mode current below 150 kHz into PE / the PV array
capacitance ≤ **30 mA rms** (10 % of the 300 mA continuous residual-current threshold of IEC 62109-2 for
transformerless PCE, so a PCS RCMU never sees the DC/DC); touch current with PE open ≤ 3.5 mA (IEC 60990 weighting).
Estimate without any choke: **0.5-16 mA rms** (three phases interleaved, duty-dependent) → met. The old requirement
(L_cm ≥ 0.5 mH at 10 kHz, |Z| ≥ 50 Ω at 16-100 kHz) was self-imposed and is dropped.

### 8.2 PV port: contactor yes, fuse no, precharge no

One HFE82V-300C/1000 in A+, because (1) it is the reverse-polarity protection (no series diode, no clamp, no loss);
(2) a PV array is a current source that no upstream device will clear: a module with a failed-short leg would be fed
up to 169 A indefinitely — the contactor isolates it (169 A ≪ 300 A); (3) it disconnects the array for the IMD
measurement and controlled stops. Cost 55.7 USD with its driver. No fuse: the array cannot exceed 169 A (below any
fuse's fusing current, `port_design` §6.5); reverse current from the module into an array fault is limited by the
converter (S1 body diode path needs S3 on → OC trip). No precharge: the array is current-limited; when the battery is
present the converter pre-charges the A bank first.

## 12. What the lean design gives up (plain list for the owner)

1. **Terminal-short survival** (48 clamp rectifiers): a bolted short at a port's terminals now probably destroys that
   port's legs; the module fails without fire (fuse / contactor) and is repaired.
2. **The PELV control island:** the controller and its debug header are at up to 1000 V from earth; service needs
   isolated probes; field updates go over CAN.
3. **The dual-channel safety chain and its fault analysis (75-row FMEA):** one latch; the external stop is a
   single-channel functional stop with no SIL/PL claim — if a cabinet needs a rated safety function, it must open its
   own DC switching devices.
4. **Self-sufficient fault coverage on the battery port:** the band 0.95-1.3 kA is left to the upstream battery
   protection (0.25 s) — §6.3. Full-range gBat fuses and the paralleled HVC43 pair are gone.
5. **The cabinet 24 V and redundant feeds:** the module is dark when both ports are below ~200 V.
6. **Features:** Ethernet, CAN FD (the MCAN exists, not wired), second CAN, second RS-485, 8 + 8 digital I/O, 12
   temperature channels, 4 fan channels → 1 CAN, 1 RS-485, 1 stop input, 1 status output, 7 (9) temperatures, 3 fans.
7. **Contactor auxiliary contact:** weld detection by a voltage test instead of a mirror contact.
8. **Active discharge:** passive bleeders, "wait 10 min" (15 min for PV-P100/110) instead of an active discharge.
9. **Margins:** Tj at 45 °C full power 78 → ~110 °C (efficiency −0.05 points at full load, derating from ~55 °C);
   gate-bias regulation ±2 % instead of the UCC14241's tighter loop and no per-channel power-good; inductor-current
   sensing 1.5 % open-loop TMR instead of 0.5 % closed-loop LEM; the fan's rated low temperature −10 °C instead of
   −40 °C; the precharge resistor 600 J instead of 2,300 J (3 attempts, then lockout); if the Faratronic second source
   is used, film capacitors rated 1200 V at 70 °C instead of 1300 V (about half the voltage-life margin, Faratronic
   only where the hot spot stays ≤ 70 °C).
10. **The common controller card** shared with other products (F28388D): the F280039C board is specific (the DAB can
    reuse it, §16).

## 15. Changes against the D-044 direction (where I refined or disagree)

1. **PV port keeps one contactor** (the direction left it open): reverse polarity and isolation of a faulted module
   from a current source that no upstream device clears are worth 56 USD.
2. **Battery port: aR fuse + a contactor that may break faults up to 1.0 kA** (HFE82V-300C/1000: 1.5 kA once at
   1000 V). This shrinks the band left upstream from "contactor breaking capacity to fuse fast region" to
   **0.95-1.3 kA within 0.25 s**.
3. **Fans and communication share one reinforced SELV domain fed by a winding of the single aux transformer**; the
   live rail needs no optocoupler. No separate isolated DC/DC module anywhere.
4. Refinements: the heatsink stays earthed (not BUS-); contactor coils are live (coil-to-mounting becomes a basic
   crossing — R-04); the IMD uses two isolated SiC SSRs (CA-IS3417WT) referenced to the live controller.
5. **Disagreements:** (a) **800 USD is not reachable while the inductor is frozen** at its 80 USD estimate; (b)
   **~1,000 parts is not reachable with the frozen gate-drive channel** (~85 parts per channel); (c) **one board is
   not justified** (§10).

## 16. DAB-D60 on the same principles

Today (`sim/out/dab_design/report.md` §0, `dab_spec.json`, `bom/COST.md`): 60 kW isolated DAB, 2 full bridges with
2 paralleled 1200 V discretes per switch (8 × IV3Q12013T4Z / SG2M014120LJ per bridge, 7.31 USD), 100 kHz, two-unit
transformer (186 USD est.) + series inductor (86 USD est.), liquid cold plate; **BOM 3,606 USD** (60 USD/kW) with
DD600N16K clamps 785, NH fuses 494, HVC43 contactors 163, flow switch 131, thermostat 69, HF MLCCs 100-317.

**What changes with the same principles:** the controller sits on **port 2's negative** (battery side); bridge-2
drivers and sensing are functional, as in the PV module. **Port 1 is a second DC system**: the transformer and every
part bridging the ports need **basic** insulation at 1850 V DC (D-032: port 1 ↔ port 2 basic, PD ≤ 10 pC at
3149 Vpk) — so bridge-1 bias needs one per-bridge transformer with a basic 1850 V DC barrier (still cheaper than
4 UCC14241), and V1 / I1 cross that barrier once (CA-IS1311BG + CA-IS3115AW for V1, one TMR sensor for I1). The
single reinforced B1 barrier to SELV stays. Both ports face stiff sources (PCS DC link, battery) → both are
battery-type ports (aR fuse pair + HFE82V + precharge). No clamp banks (−785 / −53 USD). The coolant flow switch and
the thermostat become two NTCs (inlet/outlet ΔT) with a comparator. HF capacitors: Chinese PP film (Faratronic class)
instead of 20 × 47 nF C0G MLCCs per bridge.
