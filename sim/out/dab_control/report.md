## PCS-P125 DC link (coordinator addendum, PCS control study)

Read at run time: C_dc 409.4 uF (`sim/out/pcs_design/pcs_spec.json` dc_link.C_total_uF) in parallel with the DAB's own 135 uF; PCS DC over-voltage trip 1050 V (handover limits); UV stop = the lowest V_dc for rated current 647 V at the 400 V grid, 710 V at +10 % (operating_map); PCS DC-link PI 40 Hz with its zero at f_c / 4 and the measured DC-current feed-forward, lag 114 us (its current loop + sampling delay, `sim/out/pcs_control/pcs_control_spec.json`). Bus at 750 V, one branch from an 800 V battery. The PCS is an ideal current source behind its loop (stiff grid): the weak-grid case (SCR 5) is the PCS study's (a 125 kW DC/DC trip reaches 1098 V there) and is not reproduced here. Off-grid (the DAB forms the bus): a full-power step dips the bus by 43 V at the present 400 Hz CV crossover (34 V at 500 Hz, the cascade's ceiling) - the 0.41 mF link is not too small for the present loop at the 400 V grid (707 V against 647 V). At a +10 % grid the stop is 710 V: it needs about 423 Hz (dip ~ 1/f_cv, calculated; within the 500 Hz ceiling) or - the cheap rule - a setpoint >= 752 V in off-grid operation.

A DAB trip while it forms the bus at full load: the bus reaches the UV stop after 0.70 ms (0.54 mF, 80 A, calculated); with the inverter regenerating at full power it reaches the OV trip after 2.04 ms. A PCS trip while the DAB feeds the bus: the DAB's own port-1 OV trip (1000 V, 63 us path, dab_spec) is reached after 1.70 ms and the bus peaks at 1009 V, below the PCS's 1050 V - the DAB stops first.

**DAB-side firmware requirements for the PCS-P125 link** (dab_spec firmware_requirements FW-DAB-8..10):

* FW-DAB-8 power ramp: every change of the DAB power reference into a link the PCS holds (start, stop, step, reversal) at <= 6.25 kW/ms in total - the PCS study's rule, 9.6 ms for 60 kW - shared as /N_alive between branches (system controller); the branch firmware's own 20 A/ms ramp (16 kW/ms at 800 V) is only the local limit. On a stiff grid even a step stays inside the band (747-766 V); the rule is for the weak grid (PCS study, SCR 5: collapse without it).
* FW-DAB-9 stops and trips: a non-hazard stop ramps the power to zero at the FW-DAB-8 rate before the gates go off; a hazard trip gates off at once and reports on the module bus, and the PCS takes the step out of its reference through its measured DC current (feed-forward lag 114 us: 735 / 765 V here on a stiff grid). Who opens what: the tripped DAB gates off first; its port contactors open only after its current is zero; the PCS keeps its DC contactor closed and holds the link; on a weak grid the PCS study's coordinated trip (PCS current cut in the same control frame) is the PCS-side requirement, not the DAB's.
* FW-DAB-10 off-grid bus forming (CV on port 1, the inverter grid-forming): CV crossover 400 Hz, no load feed-forward (the DAB does not measure the inverter current); when the grid is above nominal either a setpoint >= 752 V or a crossover >= 423 Hz; the DAB's port-1 OV trip (1000 V) stays below the PCS's 1050 V so that on a PCS trip the DAB stops first (above); the PCS stops on its own UV detection when the DAB trips.

## Parallel branches (DAB-09) - decision and fault behaviour

* No hardware carrier-sync line between branches (decision): carriers are free-running; the per-branch current loop makes sharing independent of carrier phase (same result with branch 2 offset by 30 deg). Ripple at the shared buses adds without interleaving and beats slowly; each branch keeps its own capacitor banks.
* Sharing = common reference over CAN-B + per-branch inner loop on the branch's own I2 sensor.
* One-branch fault: the tripped branch's current is gone within one period; the survivor holds its own reference because its loop is local. The bus transient is a GENERIC PCS (not PCS-P125: its 0.41 mF link is the section above) seeing a 60 kW load step on a 4 mF link with a 30 Hz voltage loop: 19 V with DC-current feed-forward (2 ms lag), 65 V without -> the PCS needs load-current feed-forward (or a faster loop) to stay clear of the 960 V firmware OV limit when V1 runs near 900 V; the PCS power limit is lowered in the same CAN-B frame.
* Limit the system controller must apply: total reference <= N_alive x P_branch,max(V1, V2) from the derating map, and each branch clamps its own reference to P_branch,max/V2. Without that, redistributing I_total/N_alive drives the survivor to the 100 A port limit (80 kW at 800 V, a 33 % overload) at the first CAN frame after the trip.

## What this means for the hardware

* Open-loop (common-phase) paralleling shares current in inverse proportion to L_k: a +/-5 % L tolerance gives about +/-5 % current imbalance, so each branch needs its own current measurement and inner loop; the sharing error then equals the current-sensor gain mismatch -> specify branch current sensing to <= +/-1 % gain after calibration.
* The phase update must keep volt-second balance (split update); a naive step leaves a DC offset in the transformer current that decays only with L/R (~160 us here) - the flux-balance loop and the 5 A DC trip in dab_spec.json are sized with this in mind.
* The dead-time phase drift found in dab_design (light load) is absorbed by the current loop; the feed-forward only sets the starting point.

Net: nothing in ST's firmware changes our hardware. Two firmware items are adopted as additions, not replacements: the on-line SPS P_max plausibility check, and the closed-form trapezoidal law as a plausibility reference - not as a fallback modulation: its zero-current edges are hard turn-ons with SiC, which the turn-on rule forbids above 600 V, so outside the LUT's feasible set the gates stay off (FW-DAB-1). ST's 400 ns fixed dead time and its voltage-only loop are not adopted (reasons above).

## Assumptions

* `R_loop` = 0.0428933: ohm primary-referred: 2 x R_pos(125 C) bridge 1 + n^2 x 2 x R_pos(125 C) bridge 2 + transformer + inductor R_dc + AC bus (dab_design.py)
* `R_line1` = 0.01: ohm port-1 source impedance (PCS DC bus treated as stiff)
* `R_int_batt` = 0.06: ohm battery string (240S LFP ~0.25 mohm/cell)
* `sensor_gain_err` = 0.01: per-branch current-sensor gain error used in the sharing test (+1 % / -1 %)
* `f_ci` = 2000: Hz current-loop crossover
* `f_cv` = 400: Hz voltage-loop crossover
* `phi_max_deg` = 60: deg phase-command clamp
* `phi_slew_deg` = 3: deg maximum phase change per switching period (modulator slew limit)
* `carrier_offset_deg` = 30: deg relative carrier phase of branch 2 (free-running, no sync line)
* `C_pcs` = 0.004: F GENERIC PCS DC-link capacitance on port 1 (one-branch-fault case) - not PCS-P125, whose link is read from pcs_spec.json in the PCS-P125 link cases
* `f_pcs` = 30: Hz PCS DC-link voltage-loop crossover, no load-current feed-forward
* `t_can` = 0.01: s CAN-B reference/status frame period (system controller to branches)
* `i_ramp` = 20000: A/s branch-local reference ramp (firmware), applied to every CAN reference change
* `pcs_ff_tau` = 0.002: s PCS DC-current feed-forward lag (PCS current loop + filter); None = voltage loop only
* `v_bus_pcs` = 750: V PCS-P125 DC-link operating point of the link cases (the PCS control study's V_dc)
* `dpdt_pcs` = 6.25e+06: W/s largest DC/DC power ramp into the PCS-P125 link: the PCS control study's rule (125 kW in 20 ms) - the system total, so per branch /N_alive (ASSUMED allocation)
* `p_pre_cv` = 0.1: inverter load before / after the off-grid load step, fraction of P_branch,max (ASSUMED)
* `f_cv_max` = 500: Hz highest CV crossover the cascade allows: f_ci / 4 with the one-period delay and the 3 deg per period slew (design rule, ASSUMED)
* `P_branch_max` = 60000: W per-branch power limit at 800/800 V, dab_spec.json derating_charge_W
* Ideal edges (no dead time / ZVS transitions) in this control model; switching losses not included.
* The magnetising branch is lossless here (all resistance lumped in series with L), so a few-ampere DC drift of i_m set by the start-up/operating-point history does not decay in this model; real secondary winding and switch resistance damp it (tau ~ L_m/R_sec, ms) and the flux-balance loop removes the rest.
* Port 1 is a stiff PCS bus; the PCS voltage loop is not modelled.
